A case has one lifecycle, and the words are not interchangeable

Most disputes about charitable platforms come from conflating these. A campaign reaching its target is not a payment. A payment is not an outcome.

Case states and what each one means
StateWhat is trueWhat is not yet true
SubmittedA family has sent an application and evidence.Nothing has been checked.
VerifiedIncome, invoice, school and payment destination confirmed.No decision has been taken.
ApprovedThe committee recorded a decision and a rationale.No money is reserved.
Campaign liveAn anonymised case is public and accepting donations.Nothing has been paid.
Funding completeThe target has been reached and collection has paused.The school has not been paid.
PaidTwo approvers released funds to the school's verified account.The bank has not been reconciled.
ReconciledThe payment is matched to a statement line and journalled.No educational outcome is confirmed.
ConfirmedThe school confirmed enrolment and attendance continued.Grades are never collected or published.

Separate flags cover paused, withdrawn, expired, under appeal and suspected fraud. These are states, not stages, and a case can carry one without leaving its place in the lifecycle.

What the charity does

  • Holds the fundraising permissions and the donor relationship.
  • Appoints the committee and sets eligibility policy.
  • Takes custody of donated items and contracts the specialists.
  • Owns the payment accounts and the statutory reporting.
  • Decides every case. No decision is automated.

What the technology provider does

  • Builds and runs the platform, and keeps it available and secure.
  • Integrates the services the charity has approved.
  • Works under charity-approved data-processing terms.
  • Holds no payment account and touches no charitable funds.
  • Has support access that is limited, time-boxed and logged.
